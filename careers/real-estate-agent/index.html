Real Estate Agent

Real estate agents help people buy, sell, and lease homes and commercial properties. They market listings, show properties, negotiate offers, and guide clients through the paperwork of a deal. In Ontario they work as independent contractors under a registered brokerage and are paid by commission, so income depends on closing deals.

What does a Real Estate Agent do?

Days are split between a brokerage office (paperwork, calls, marketing) and being out in the field, showing homes, hosting open houses, and meeting clients, so a car and a phone are effectively the office.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

Finish high school

An Ontario Secondary School Diploma or equivalent (GED, CAEC) is required for admission to the real-estate program; applicants without one can write the program admissions exam. You must also be at least 18 and legally entitled to work in Canada.

Complete the pre-registration Real Estate Salesperson Program

Five online courses plus two in-person or virtual simulation sessions and exams, offered by RECO-designated providers (Humber Polytechnic, Fleming College, Algonquin College, Career College Group). Must be completed within 24 months of starting.

Register with RECO

Apply to the Real Estate Council of Ontario as a salesperson, clear a criminal record and judicial matters check (CRJMC), and arrange errors-and-omissions insurance. Registration is mandatory before trading in real estate in Ontario.

Join a registered brokerage

Sign on with a brokerage as an independent contractor under a broker of record. Complete the post-registration phase of the Salesperson Program within two years of registering, plus ongoing continuing education.

Progress to broker licence

After at least 24 of the previous 36 months as a registered salesperson, complete the Real Estate Broker Program to become a broker, the step required to open your own brokerage.

Licensing: Registration with the Real Estate Council of Ontario (RECO) is mandatory, it is illegal to trade in real estate in Ontario without it. Registration requires completing the pre-registration Salesperson Program through a RECO-designated education provider, passing the exams, clearing a criminal record check, and working under a registered brokerage.

Stress level

High

Pay is 100% commission-based, so income swings with the market and deal flow, and 86% of Ontario agents are self-employed with no guaranteed paycheque. Agents also work to client deadlines and availability, including evenings and weekends, which keeps pressure high.

Work-life balance

Challenging

As a contractor you set your own calendar, but clients expect evenings and weekends for showings, and offer deadlines are non-negotiable. The feast-or-famine rhythm of commission income means many agents work long hours when business is hot.

Flexibility

  • Remote workMostly in-person, showings, open houses, inspections, and closings happen at properties; paperwork, marketing, and client calls can be done from home.
  • SchedulingVery flexible in theory, you choose your hours, but client availability (evenings and weekends) and deal deadlines dictate the real schedule.
  • FreelanceAgents work as independent contractors under a registered brokerage, not as employees, and earn commission splits on each deal.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Prospect for new clients through networking, referrals, and online leads
  • List properties: take photos, arrange staging, and publish listings on MLS and portals
  • Host open houses and schedule private showings for buyers
  • Prepare comparative market analyses to advise clients on pricing
  • Draft and present offers to purchase, and negotiate terms between buyers and sellers
  • Coordinate inspections, appraisals, and closing paperwork with lawyers and lenders
  • Maintain a client database and follow up with past clients for repeat business and referrals
  • Stay current on local market trends and complete RECO continuing education

Where it can lead

  • Newly registered salesperson learning the trade under a brokerage's broker of record
  • Experienced agent with a steady referral network and repeat clients
  • Team leader or mentor managing junior agents
  • Licensed broker supervising a brokerage's agents
  • Brokerage owner/manager or specialist in commercial, luxury, or investment properties
