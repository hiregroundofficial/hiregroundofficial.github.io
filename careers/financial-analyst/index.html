Financial Analyst

Financial analysts study companies, markets, and economic data to help businesses and investors make smart money decisions. They build financial models, analyze earnings reports, and recommend investments or business strategies. It is a numbers-heavy, deadline-driven career at the heart of banking, investing, and corporate finance.

What does a Financial Analyst do?

Most financial analysts work in offices at banks, investment firms, pension funds, or corporate finance departments, clustered heavily in Toronto's financial district, often under deadline pressure tied to market hours and reporting cycles.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school

Take math, English, business studies, and economics; join finance or investing clubs and practice spreadsheets.

University (4 years)

Bachelor's degree in finance, economics, business, or accounting. Seek co-op placements in banks or investment firms.

Entry-level analyst role

Start as an analyst; learn financial modeling, valuation, and reporting cycles on the job.

Specialize (2–4 years in)

Choose a track: equity research, investment banking, portfolio management, or corporate finance.

Advanced credentials

Take CFA exams, earn an MBA, or pursue advanced credentials while working to reach senior roles.

Required qualifications
  • ✓Bachelor's degree in finance, economics, business, accounting, or a related field (typical employer requirement, not legally mandated)
Common qualifications
  • ○Bachelor's degree in finance, economics, business administration, accounting, or mathematics/statistics
  • ○Internship or co-op experience in banking, finance, or investment management
  • ○Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) charter or progress toward it, for investment-side roles
Optional qualifications
  • +CFA charter (Chartered Financial Analyst)
  • +MBA or master's degree in finance, economics, or a quantitative field
  • +Additional exams or certificates such as FRM (Financial Risk Manager) for risk roles
Licensing: There is no mandatory license required to work as a financial analyst in Ontario. The CFA charter is a respected but optional professional credential for investment-side roles.

Stress level

High

Analysts face tight market-driven deadlines, quarterly earnings-season crunches with very long hours, and high-pressure demands for accurate forecasts; researchers describe them as having a professionally aggressive style and a higher tolerance for stress.

Work-life balance

Challenging

Long and unpredictable hours are common, especially during quarterly earnings seasons and active deal periods; corporate finance roles tend to offer steadier schedules than investment banking or equity research.

Flexibility

  • Remote workMostly office-based, especially at banks and funds in Toronto; some hybrid arrangements are becoming available for corporate finance roles.
  • SchedulingSchedules are generally rigid with long or irregular hours during earnings seasons and deal closings.
  • FreelanceIndependent freelance financial analysis work is uncommon; occasional consulting for startups is possible.

Experiences & activities

  • Practice building financial models and spreadsheets in Excel or Google Sheets
  • Follow the stock market and read company annual reports
  • Join a school investment or finance club, or paper-trade a virtual portfolio
  • Take an online course in accounting or corporate finance fundamentals
  • Seek a summer job, co-op, or internship at a bank or financial firm

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Building financial models in Excel to forecast company revenue, costs, and cash flows
  • Analyzing quarterly financial statements, earnings releases, and industry trends
  • Writing investment research reports with buy, hold, or sell recommendations
  • Presenting findings and recommendations to fund managers, portfolio managers, or senior executives
  • Tracking economic indicators, market data, and regulatory developments that affect valuations
  • Supporting mergers, acquisitions, capital-raising, and corporate budgeting processes
  • Communicating complex financial findings clearly to clients or non-financial stakeholders

Where it can lead

  • Analyst / Associate (entry-level financial or research analyst)
  • Senior analyst / Associate, equity research or investment banking
  • Vice President / Portfolio manager / Lead analyst
  • Director / Managing director / Chief investment officer or fund principal
