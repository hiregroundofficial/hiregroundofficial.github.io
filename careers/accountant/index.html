Accountant (CPA)

Accountants keep the financial records of businesses, governments, and non-profits accurate and honest. They prepare financial statements, file tax returns, conduct audits, and help leaders make smart money decisions. A Chartered Professional Accountant (CPA) is Canada's top accounting designation, it's the gold-standard credential that opens the most doors in the field.

What does an Accountant (CPA) do?

Most accountants work in offices at public accounting firms, corporate finance departments, banks, government agencies, or non-profits, using accounting software and spreadsheets. Auditors and client-facing practitioners regularly travel to client sites; audit fieldwork and tax-season spikes shape the daily rhythm.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school (Grades 9–12)

Focus on math (Advanced Functions, Calculus & Vectors) and business studies, including accounting courses if offered. Build Excel and financial literacy skills early.

Bachelor's degree (4 years)

University degree in accounting or a related business discipline, covering the prerequisite courses required for CPA PEP entry: financial reporting, management accounting, audit and assurance, finance, and taxation.

CPA Professional Education Program (CPA PEP) (~2 years)

Graduate-level professional modules taken while working, covering core competencies in accounting, assurance, finance, and tax.

Practical experience (30 months)

30 months of approved accounting work experience, typically completed concurrently with PEP, under the supervision of a CPA mentor.

Common Final Examination (CFE) + admission

Pass the national 3-day CFE, then apply for membership with CPA Ontario to earn the CPA designation.

Required qualifications
  • ✓University degree in accounting or a related discipline (required for CPA pathway entry)
  • ✓Completion of the CPA Professional Education Program (PEP), including 30 months of approved practical experience and a pass on the Common Final Examination
  • ✓CPA Ontario membership to use the CPA title in Ontario
  • ✓Public accounting licence (through CPA Ontario) for anyone practising public accounting, e.g. signing audited financial statements
Common qualifications
  • ○Degree from a program that already covers all CPA PEP prerequisite courses (e.g. accredited accounting BBA/BCom)
  • ○Advanced Excel and accounting software skills (QuickBooks, Sage, Xero)
  • ○Knowledge of Canadian tax law, ASPE/IFRS financial reporting standards, and payroll regulations
Optional qualifications
  • +Post-designation specializations: public accounting licence, CBV (Chartered Business Valuator), CFP, or tax specialization
  • +Master's degree in accounting, taxation, or finance
  • +Data analytics / data visualization tooling (Power BI, Tableau)
Licensing: CPA Ontario is the provincial regulatory body: you must be a member to use the CPA title in Ontario. Membership and licensure are separate processes, a public accounting licence is additionally required for members who practise public accounting (e.g. signing financial statements).

Optional: Students can reduce PEP coursework by completing an accredited accounting degree that covers the prerequisite courses.

Job demand

Moderate

Employment growth will create a few new positions and retirements will open more; demand driven by ongoing need for accounting/auditing services, financial decision-making, and regulatory oversight. Demand spikes during tax-filing season.

Stress level

Moderate

Work follows predictable cycles but with hard, non-negotiable deadlines, the April 30 tax-filing deadline and fiscal year-end/audit closes create intense periods where Canadian accounting staff typically work 60–65 hours per week, including Saturdays. Outside busy seasons, hours are generally normal and the work is steady.

Work-life balance

Challenging

Tax season (roughly February–April) makes balance very difficult, with long hours and weekend work common in public practice; the rest of the year is typically standard 40-hour weeks. Industry and government roles generally offer better balance than public accounting firms.

Flexibility

  • Remote workRemote and hybrid arrangements are increasingly common, especially in corporate finance, government, and bookkeeping roles; auditors still need on-site time for client fieldwork.
  • SchedulingFirms often allow flex time and compressed schedules outside busy season, but overtime during tax season and audit deadlines is typically mandatory, not optional.
  • FreelanceSelf-employment is common, many CPAs run their own bookkeeping, tax preparation, or advisory practices, and contract/seasonal tax work is widespread.

  • Calculus and Vectors
  • Business Studies / Accounting (where offered)
  • English (report writing and communication)
  • Computer studies (spreadsheets and data tools)

Experiences & activities

  • Learn Excel to an advanced level (formulas, pivot tables, modelling)
  • Volunteer as treasurer for a school club or community group to practice real bookkeeping
  • Seek summer or co-op placements in accounting firms, banks, or finance departments
  • Do personal tax returns for family members to learn the Canadian tax basics
  • Join business/accounting clubs and case competitions at school

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Prepare financial statements (income statements, balance sheets, cash flow statements)
  • File personal and corporate tax returns and advise clients on tax planning
  • Conduct audits of client financial records and internal controls
  • Reconcile bank accounts, ledgers, and intercompany transactions
  • Process payroll and ensure compliance with CRA payroll rules
  • Build budgets and analyze variances between actual and planned results
  • Perform financial analysis to support business decisions (pricing, investment, cost control)
  • Maintain and close the general ledger at month- and year-end

Where it can lead

  • Junior / Staff Accountant, bookkeeping, reconciliations, assisting audits under supervision
  • Senior Accountant, lead audit engagements, review work, handle complex tax files
  • Accounting Manager / Supervisor, manage teams, budgets, and client portfolios
  • Controller or Director of Finance, own an organization's full accounting function
  • Partner (public practice) or CFO (industry), strategic leadership of the firm or company's finances
