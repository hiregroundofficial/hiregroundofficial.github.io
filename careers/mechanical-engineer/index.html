Mechanical Engineer

Mechanical engineers design and build the machines and systems that move the world, engines, robots, HVAC systems, manufacturing equipment, and more. They use physics, math, and computer modelling to design parts, test prototypes, and make sure equipment works safely and efficiently.

What does a Mechanical Engineer do?

Engineering offices, design studios, and manufacturing plants, with occasional visits to test labs, factories, or client sites. Mostly computer-based design and analysis work combined with hands-on testing.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma with Advanced Functions, Calculus and Vectors, Physics, Chemistry, and English. High marks matter, engineering programs are competitive.

University

Complete a 4-year bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering, ideally from a CEAB-accredited program (the standard route to a P.Eng. licence). Co-op terms provide paid experience and connections.

Engineering Experience

Work as an engineer-in-training (EIT) under the supervision of a licensed engineer, building the required professional experience.

P.Eng. Licence

Meet PEO's requirements: acceptable engineering education, about 48 months of experience, and the professional practice examination. Once licensed, you can practise professional engineering independently and sign off on engineering work.

Career Progression

Advance to senior engineer, project manager, technical specialist, or engineering management. Some move into consulting, sales engineering, or executive roles.

Education time

4 years to enter the field; about 8 years to P.Eng.
  • •High school: Ontario Secondary School Diploma with university-track math and sciences (4 years)
  • •University: 4-year bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering
  • •Licensing: about 4 years of engineering experience after graduation to qualify for the P.Eng. licence

Mandatory: A bachelor's degree is the entry route to employment. The P.Eng. licence is mandatory only to independently practise professional engineering and take responsibility for engineering work in Ontario.

Optional: Master's and doctoral degrees are optional and suit research, teaching, or highly specialized roles.

Job demand

Limited

Employment decline is expected for the broader engineering group and prospects are constrained by weak manufacturing conditions, though retirements will open some positions.

Stress level

Moderate

Project deadlines, tight budgets, and responsibility for designs where failure can cause injury or major costs create sustained pressure. Manufacturing-sector uncertainty adds job-security stress for some engineers.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Standard full-time hours are typical, but overtime is common around project deadlines and product launches. Hybrid work options help some engineers balance schedules.

Flexibility

  • Remote workCommon for design and analysis roles, hybrid arrangements are widespread in consulting and product companies.
  • SchedulingStandard office hours; overtime around project deadlines and product launches.
  • FreelancePossible, consulting engineering exists, but requires a P.Eng. licence and a Certificate of Authorization.

  • Apply for engineering co-op programs at university
  • Build and document personal engineering projects

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Design mechanical parts and systems using 3D CAD software
  • Run simulations and stress analysis on designs
  • Build and test prototypes, then refine them
  • Analyze failures and find root causes of equipment problems
  • Prepare technical drawings, specifications, and reports
  • Work with manufacturers to make designs production-ready
  • Manage project schedules, budgets, and safety requirements

Where it can lead

  • Engineering Graduate / EIT
  • Licensed Professional Engineer (P.Eng.)
  • Senior Mechanical Engineer
  • Project Manager or Technical Specialist
  • Engineering Manager or Director
