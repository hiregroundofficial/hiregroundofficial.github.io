Data Analyst

Data analysts turn raw numbers into useful information. They collect, clean, and study data, like sales figures or website traffic, and present their findings in charts and reports so organizations can make better decisions.

What does a Data Analyst do?

Analysts work in offices at banks, tech firms, retailers, government, and hospitals, or remotely from home. They spend most of the day at a computer working with data, and meet with teams to share findings.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Focus on math (functions, statistics, calculus), computer science, and English. Strong math and clear communication skills are the foundation of this career.

Postsecondary Education

Employers usually require a bachelor's degree, often in computer science, computer engineering, or mathematics, or a college program in a related field. Some analysts add a postgraduate certificate in data analytics.

Gain Experience

Practice with real datasets (Kaggle competitions, personal projects with SQL and dashboards) and complete co-op or internship terms. Programming and related experience is usually required.

Entry-Level Position

Start as a junior or associate analyst building reports and dashboards, usually working with a data or business team.

Career Progression

Advance to senior analyst or analytics manager roles, or branch into data science (with further study in statistics/machine learning) or business intelligence engineering.

Mandatory: A degree or college program plus programming experience is typically required by employers. No government licence is needed, this occupation is not regulated in Canada.

Optional: Certifications and data-science graduate study are optional and mainly help with senior or specialized roles.

Stress level

Moderate

Reporting deadlines and requests from many departments can create busy periods, and presenting findings to decision-makers carries responsibility. Day-to-day work is mostly desk-based analysis rather than crisis response.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Regular full-time hours are typical; overtime can occur around reporting deadlines or major projects but is not usually constant.

Flexibility

  • Remote workCommon, data analysts frequently work remote or hybrid.
  • SchedulingUsually standard business hours.
  • FreelancePossible (project-based analytics consulting) but less common than full-time roles.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Collect and clean data from different sources
  • Write SQL queries to pull information
  • Build dashboards and reports
  • Analyze trends and patterns in data
  • Present findings to managers or teams
  • Check data for errors and quality issues
  • Automate routine reports
