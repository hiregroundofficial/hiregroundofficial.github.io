Pediatrician

Pediatricians are doctors who specialize in the health of babies, children, and teenagers, from treating ear infections and asthma to tracking growth and development and vaccinating. They work closely with worried parents, which takes patience and strong communication. Note: Job Bank groups pediatricians with all clinical and laboratory medical specialists (NOC 31100) for salary and outlook data below.

What does a Pediatrician do?

Children's hospitals, clinics, and community practices. Work is patient-facing and family-centred, combining examinations, procedures, hospital rounds, and parent counselling.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Senior biology, chemistry, and English with excellent grades. Volunteer with children (camps, tutoring, hospitals) to confirm you enjoy working with kids.

Undergraduate Degree

Complete a 4-year bachelor's degree and write the MCAT.

Medical School

Complete a 4-year Doctor of Medicine (MD) degree.

Residency

Complete a 4-year pediatrics residency (core pediatric training, including newborn, emergency, and inpatient care) and pass the MCCQE exams.

Certification and Licensing

Pass RCPSC pediatrics specialty exams and obtain a CPSO licence.

Entry-Level Position

Practise as a general pediatrician in a clinic, children's hospital, or community setting.

Career Progression

Pursue a subspecialty fellowship (e.g., neonatology, pediatric emergency medicine, pediatric cardiology), become a department chief, or take an academic role.

Stress level

High

High stress from treating seriously ill children, emotional pressure from anxious parents, on-call emergency coverage, and the responsibility of diagnosing patients who cannot always describe their symptoms.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Community pediatricians often keep regular office hours, but hospital-based pediatricians face on-call nights and weekends that strain balance.

Flexibility

  • Remote workLimited, examinations require in-person visits, though some follow-ups and consultations can be virtual.
  • SchedulingModerate control in community practice; hospital roles follow set call schedules.
  • FreelanceAlmost 60% of clinical specialists in Ontario are self-employed; private clinic practice is common.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Examine infants, children, and teenagers
  • Diagnose and treat childhood illnesses and injuries
  • Track growth, development, and milestones
  • Administer vaccinations and preventive screenings
  • Counsel parents on nutrition, behaviour, and development
  • Order and interpret lab tests and imaging
  • Refer complex cases to pediatric subspecialists
  • Round on patients in children's hospital wards

Where it can lead

  • Pediatrics resident
  • General pediatrician (clinic, hospital, or community)
  • Subspecialist (e.g., neonatology, pediatric emergency, pediatric cardiology)
  • Department chief or medical director
  • Academic pediatrician (teaching and research)
