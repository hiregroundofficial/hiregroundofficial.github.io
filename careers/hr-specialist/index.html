Human Resources Specialist

Human Resources Specialists develop, implement and evaluate HR policies and programs, and advise employers and employees on people-related matters. They handle recruitment, onboarding, employee relations, training, compensation and labour relations. They work across nearly every industry, from government and hospitals to tech companies and manufacturers.

What does a Human Resources Specialist do?

HR Specialists work in office settings across nearly every industry, including government departments, hospitals, banks, manufacturers, and consulting firms. Work is desk-based and people-facing, meetings, interviews, and confidential conversations, with growing hybrid and remote options.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school (Grades 9–12)

Focus on English, math, business studies, and social sciences; build communication and leadership skills through clubs, student council, or part-time jobs.

Post-secondary education

Complete a university degree or college diploma in human resources management or a related field (business administration, industrial relations, commerce, psychology), or an HR professional development program. Typically 2–4 years.

Entry-level HR experience

Start in roles like HR assistant, HR coordinator, or recruiter to learn day-to-day operations, screening candidates, maintaining employee records, and supporting onboarding.

HRPA registration and coursework (optional)

Register with HRPA and complete the nine-subject coursework requirement (HR management, compensation, labour relations, recruitment and selection, etc.) as the first step toward the CHRP designation.

CHRP exams and designation (optional)

Pass the CHRP Knowledge Exam, the CHRP Employment Law Exam, and complete HRPA's Job Ready Program to earn the Certified Human Resources Professional designation, a valued asset, not a legal requirement.

Specialization and advancement

With experience, specialize in areas like talent acquisition, compensation and benefits, labour relations, or organizational development, and advance to HR manager or senior HR partner roles.

Required qualifications
  • ✓A university degree or college diploma in human resources management or a related field (business administration, industrial relations, commerce, psychology), or completion of a professional development program in HR administration
Common qualifications
  • ○Bachelor's degree in HR management or business administration
  • ○2+ years of experience in HR coordination, recruitment, or employee relations
  • ○Knowledge of Ontario employment legislation (Employment Standards Act, Human Rights Code, Occupational Health and Safety Act)
  • ○Experience with HRIS software and payroll systems
Optional qualifications
  • +CHRP (Certified Human Resources Professional) designation via HRPA, voluntary but frequently listed as an asset in job postings
  • +CHRL (Certified Human Resources Leader) for senior HR roles
  • +Certificate in labour relations, conflict resolution, or workplace investigations
  • +Bilingualism (English/French) for roles in government or national organizations
Licensing: No legal licence or certification is required to work in human resources in Ontario, HR is not a regulated profession there. The CHRP designation from the Human Resources Professionals Association (HRPA) is a voluntary professional credential that many employers value but is not compulsory to practise.

Mandatory: A university degree or college diploma in HR or a related field, or completion of an HR professional development program, is typically required by employers per the National Occupational Classification.

Optional: The CHRP designation requires nine approved courses (or the Alternate Route), the CHRP Knowledge Exam, the Employment Law Exam, and HRPA's Job Ready Program; it is an asset but not legally required.

Job demand

Limited

Employment growth will add a few new positions and not many will open from retirements; there is moderate competition from unemployed workers with recent experience, and outsourcing of HR functions to consultancies may moderate in-house job growth. Recent trends (2022–2024) were balanced.

Stress level

Moderate

HR Specialists handle emotionally charged situations such as terminations, grievances, and workplace conflicts, and face compliance deadlines around employment law and labour regulations. The workload is mostly predictable office hours, but hiring crunches, investigations, and employee crises can create intense, high-stakes periods.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Most HR roles follow standard business hours, Job Bank reports 94% of HR professionals in Ontario work full time, so evenings are generally free. However, recruitment deadlines, employee emergencies, and bargaining or restructuring periods can spill into extra hours.

Flexibility

  • Remote workModerately remote-friendly: recruitment, HRIS administration, and advisory work are often offered as hybrid or fully remote; employee relations and investigations still benefit from in-person presence.
  • SchedulingTypically standard weekday business hours with limited shift work; some flexibility around core hours is common in office-based employers.
  • FreelanceSelf-employment is possible, Job Bank notes HR professionals may be self-employed, particularly as independent HR consultants, recruiters, or contract HR advisors. Freelance/contract work is common but the majority hold full-time employee roles.

Recommended preparation (suggestions, not requirements)

High-school courses

  • Business studies / entrepreneurship
  • English (written and oral communication)
  • Mathematics / data management
  • Social sciences (psychology, law)
  • Computer / information technology courses

Experiences & activities

  • Take business and psychology courses to build people-management foundations
  • Develop strong writing and public-speaking skills for interviews, policies, and presentations
  • Gain part-time or volunteer experience involving people, teamwork, or customer service
  • Learn the basics of spreadsheets and HR databases (HRIS familiarity)
  • Join a business or leadership club (DECA, student council) to practise organizing people
  • Research HRPA's student resources and Ontario college/university HR programs

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Screen résumés and conduct job interviews with candidates
  • Coordinate onboarding, orientation, and benefits enrolment for new hires
  • Advise managers and employees on policies, performance issues, and discipline
  • Investigate workplace complaints, grievances, and harassment reports confidentially
  • Maintain personnel records and HRIS data, and run workforce reports
  • Administer compensation, benefits plans, and leave requests
  • Develop and deliver training programs (safety, diversity, onboarding)
  • Support terminations and layoffs with proper documentation and legal compliance

Where it can lead

  • HR Assistant / HR Coordinator, entry-level administrative and recruiting support
  • Human Resources Specialist / HR Generalist, own recruitment, employee relations, and policy work
  • Senior HR Advisor / HR Business Partner, advise leadership and lead strategic people initiatives
  • HR Manager, lead the HR team and oversee all HR functions for a department or site
  • Director / VP of Human Resources or CHRL/CHRE, set organization-wide people strategy
