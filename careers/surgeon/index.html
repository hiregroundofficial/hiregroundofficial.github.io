Surgeon

Surgeons diagnose conditions that need operations and perform surgery to treat injuries, disease, and deformities, from appendectomies to joint replacements. About 4,050 specialists in surgery work in Ontario, mostly in hospitals, and the work carries enormous responsibility since decisions are often life-or-death. It is one of the longest training paths in medicine.

What does a Surgeon do?

Hospitals (50%) and ambulatory care/surgical centres (49%). Work is highly team-based, surgeons operate with nurses, anesthesiologists, and surgical staff, in fast-paced, high-pressure settings.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Senior biology, chemistry, physics, and English with excellent grades. Volunteer in health care and develop fine-motor skills and composure under pressure.

Undergraduate Degree

Complete a 4-year bachelor's degree and write the MCAT.

Medical School

Complete a 4-year Doctor of Medicine (MD) degree.

Residency

Complete approximately 5 years of residency in a surgical specialty (e.g., general surgery, orthopedic surgery) and pass the Medical Council of Canada qualifying exams.

Certification and Licensing

Pass Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada (RCPSC) specialty exams and obtain a CPSO licence.

Entry-Level Position

Join a hospital surgical department as a staff surgeon, performing scheduled and emergency operations.

Career Progression

Pursue a subspecialty fellowship (e.g., trauma, cardiothoracic), become a division chief, or take an academic/teaching role at a medical school.

Stress level

High

Very high stress from life-and-death decisions, long and unpredictable hours, emergency on-call duties, physically demanding operations, and the high stakes of surgical complications.

Work-life balance

Challenging

On-call nights and weekends, emergency cases, and long operating days make balance difficult, especially early in the career.

Flexibility

  • Remote workMinimal, surgery is hands-on. Some consultations and follow-ups can be virtual.
  • SchedulingLimited control; operating schedules and on-call rosters are set by the hospital.
  • Freelance64% of surgical specialists are self-employed, typically billing fee-for-service rather than working set employee hours.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Perform scheduled and emergency operations
  • Assess patients and decide whether surgery is needed
  • Review lab results and medical imaging before procedures
  • Lead the surgical team in the operating room
  • Monitor patients during recovery and manage post-op care
  • Round on patients in hospital wards
  • Consult with referring physicians and specialists
  • Document operative notes and follow-up plans

Where it can lead

  • Surgical resident
  • Staff surgeon in a hospital department
  • Subspecialist (e.g., trauma, transplant, pediatric surgery)
  • Division chief or department head
  • Academic surgeon (teaching, research, program leadership)
