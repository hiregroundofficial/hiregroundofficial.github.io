Electrician

Electricians install, maintain, and repair the electrical wiring and equipment that powers homes, businesses, and factories. They read blueprints, run wires through walls and conduits, connect panels and fixtures, and troubleshoot problems when the power isn't working.

What does an Electrician do?

Construction sites (new buildings, renovations, industrial projects), existing homes and commercial buildings for service calls, and maintenance departments of factories. Work is indoor and outdoor, sometimes at heights or in cramped spaces.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma with math (algebra, geometry), physics, and English. Join the Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program (OYAP) or a co-op placement if your school offers one, it counts toward apprenticeship hours.

Find a Sponsor and Register

Get hired by an electrical contractor or company willing to sponsor you, then register a Training Agreement with Skilled Trades Ontario.

Apprenticeship

Complete about 9,000 hours of combined on-the-job training (roughly 7,950 hours) and in-school sessions (roughly 1,050 hours, in four levels), typically about 5 years.

Certification

Pass the Certificate of Qualification exam (70%+) for 309A Construction and Maintenance Electrician. This is a compulsory trade, you need the certificate to work unsupervised or run your own shop.

Career Progression

As a journeyperson you can specialize (industrial, commercial, residential, fire alarm, renewables), become a foreperson or project estimator, start your own contracting business, or move into inspection and training roles.

Required qualifications
  • ✓Certificate of Qualification as a 309A Construction and Maintenance Electrician, legally required to practise the trade unsupervised in Ontario (compulsory trade, Skilled Trades Ontario)
Common qualifications
  • ○Ontario Secondary School Diploma (Grade 10 is the minimum apprenticeship entry; most employers prefer Grade 12)
  • ○A registered apprenticeship with an employer sponsor
  • ○Valid driver's licence, many jobs involve travel between sites
Optional qualifications
  • +Red Seal endorsement on the Certificate of Qualification, improves mobility across provinces
  • +Pre-apprenticeship or college electrical techniques program
  • +Additional certifications (e.g. fire alarm, renewable energy systems)
Licensing: Compulsory trade: Certificate of Qualification (309A Construction and Maintenance Electrician) from Skilled Trades Ontario is legally required to work unsupervised. Voluntary Red Seal endorsement available.

Stress level

Moderate

The work is physically demanding, climbing, lifting, and working in tight or awkward spaces, and carries real safety risks from live electrical systems, so constant focus on procedures is required. Project deadlines on construction schedules add time pressure.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Hours are fairly regular on construction projects, but overtime and weekend work are common when deadlines are tight. Service electricians may rotate on-call duty for emergencies.

Flexibility

  • Remote workNot possible, the work is hands-on at physical sites.
  • SchedulingFairly regular daytime hours on most projects, with overtime during busy phases.
  • FreelancePossible for certified journeypersons (own contracting business), though most electricians are employees; about 10% of electricians are self-employed (Job Bank).

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Read and interpret blueprints and electrical drawings
  • Install wiring, outlets, lighting, and panels in new buildings
  • Troubleshoot and repair faulty circuits and equipment
  • Test systems for safe operation before energizing
  • Upgrade electrical panels and fixtures in existing buildings
  • Follow the Ontario Electrical Safety Code on every job
  • Coordinate with other trades on construction sites

Where it can lead

  • Electrical Apprentice
  • Journeyperson Electrician (309A)
  • Senior / Lead Electrician
  • Foreperson, Estimator, or Contractor
  • Inspector or Trade Instructor
