UX Designer

UX (user experience) designers make apps and websites easy and enjoyable to use. They research what users need, sketch and test designs, and work with developers to turn ideas into products people love.

What does an UX Designer do?

UX designers work at desks in offices at tech companies, agencies, banks, or startups, or remotely. They collaborate closely with product managers, developers, and researchers, and spend time interviewing and observing users.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Take art, computer science, psychology, and English. Start a small portfolio: redesign a confusing app screen and explain your thinking.

Postsecondary Education

Many UX designers complete a bachelor's degree in design, human-computer interaction (HCI), psychology, or a related field, others enter through a college diploma in graphic or interaction design plus specialized UX training.

Build a Portfolio

A portfolio of case studies (research → designs → testing → results) matters more than anything. Complete personal projects, hackathons, or bootcamp-style UX courses.

Entry-Level Position

Start as a junior UX or product designer, often supporting user research and wireframing on a design team.

Career Progression

Advance to senior designer, then UX lead or product design manager. Some specialize in UX research, interaction design, or design systems.

Education time

2–4 years
  • •Postsecondary: 4-year bachelor's degree or 2–3 year college diploma in a design-related field
  • •Portfolio: built during school or through personal projects and internships

Mandatory: There is no single mandatory credential; employers generally expect postsecondary education plus a portfolio showing real design work. No government licence is required.

Optional: UX bootcamps and HCI specializations are optional but can strengthen applications.

Job demand

Very limited

Job Bank reports a labour surplus (more workers than openings); employment decline is expected and few positions will come from retirements. Job Bank groups UX designers with graphic designers and illustrators (NOC 52120).

Stress level

Moderate

Project deadlines, rounds of user testing, and stakeholder feedback cycles create pressure. Design decisions are subjective, so designers regularly revise their work under time constraints.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Standard full-time hours are common; crunch can happen before launches, but overtime is less constant than in some tech roles.

Flexibility

  • Remote workCommon, UX design is often done remote or hybrid.
  • SchedulingGenerally standard hours with some flexibility.
  • FreelanceCommon, freelance and contract UX work is widespread.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Interview and survey users to understand needs
  • Create wireframes and interactive prototypes
  • Run usability tests and analyze results
  • Iterate designs based on feedback
  • Collaborate with developers on implementation
  • Maintain design systems and style guides
  • Present design decisions to stakeholders

Where it can lead
