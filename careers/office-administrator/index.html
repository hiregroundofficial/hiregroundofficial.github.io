Office Administrator

Office administrators keep an office running smoothly behind the scenes. They organize schedules and records, manage supplies and budgets, and help staff and customers get what they need. It is a people-and-organization job found in almost every industry, from clinics and schools to construction firms and government offices.

What does an Office Administrator do?

Work is centred in offices across almost all industries, health care, construction, government, education, and private businesses. The role involves sitting at a computer, greeting visitors, and coordinating with staff throughout the organization.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school (Grades 9–12)

Focus on business studies, English, and computer/technology courses; build typing speed and basic spreadsheet skills.

College diploma (1–2 years)

Complete an Office Administration or Business Administration diploma at an Ontario college, the typical entry pathway.

Work-integrated learning

Co-op placement or internship during the diploma to gain real office experience and employer contacts.

Entry-level admin role

Start as an administrative assistant or receptionist to learn procedures and software on the job.

Optional certifications

Add credentials such as Microsoft Office Specialist or CAP (Certified Administrative Professional) to stand out.

Advance

Move up to senior administrative officer or office manager, supervising other admin staff.

  • +Microsoft Office Specialist (MOS) certification

  • +Bookkeeping or accounting fundamentals certificate
Licensing: No licence is required to work as an office administrator in Ontario. Employers set their own education and experience requirements, typically a college diploma or equivalent office experience.

Stress level

Moderate

The role involves juggling competing schedules, deadlines, and interruptions while being the go-to person for staff and visitors. Front-desk and multi-tasking pressure can spike during busy periods, but hours are usually predictable with limited after-hours demands.

Work-life balance

Strong

Most roles follow standard weekday business hours with evenings and weekends off. Job Bank data shows 83% of administrative officers in Ontario work full-time and the work is overwhelmingly year-round, on-site office work.

Flexibility

  • Remote workPartially remote-friendly, some employers allow hybrid schedules, but many roles are on-site because they handle physical mail, visitors, and equipment.
  • SchedulingGenerally fixed daytime hours with limited shift or evening work; part-time roles exist (17% of Ontario workers in this occupation).
  • FreelanceSelf-employment is uncommon in this occupation (only about 8% of Ontario administrative officers are self-employed).

  • Learn professional email and phone etiquette
  • Practice organizing events, files, and schedules

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Schedule appointments and manage calendars for managers or teams
  • Prepare reports, memos, invoices, and other business documents
  • Greet visitors and answer phones and emails
  • Maintain filing systems and digital records
  • Order office supplies and manage inventory
  • Coordinate meetings, travel arrangements, and office events
  • Track expenses and assist with basic budgeting or payroll paperwork
  • Implement and improve office procedures and workflows

Where it can lead

  • Administrative assistant / receptionist
  • Administrative officer / office administrator
  • Senior administrative officer / executive assistant
  • Office manager or supervisor of admin staff
  • Operations coordinator or administrative services manager
