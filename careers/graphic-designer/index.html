Graphic Designer

Graphic designers create the visuals that communicate ideas, logos, posters, packaging, social media graphics, and layouts for print and screens. They combine art, typography, and technology to make messages clear and memorable.

What does a Graphic Designer do?

Designers work in offices at agencies, publishers, and in-house marketing teams, or remotely from home studios. About half work in professional, scientific, and technical services; many others are in media and entertainment.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Take visual arts, media studies, and computer courses. Start a portfolio now: design posters for school events, logos for clubs, or graphics for social media.

Postsecondary Education

A university degree in visual arts with a graphic design specialization OR a college diploma in graphic arts is required (Job Bank / NOC 52120). Many Ontario colleges offer 2–3 year graphic design programs.

Build a Portfolio

Creative ability demonstrated by a portfolio of work is required. Include varied projects, branding, layouts, digital graphics, and document your design process.

Entry-Level Position

Start as a junior designer at an agency, in-house marketing team, or as a freelancer doing small client projects.

Career Progression

Advance to senior designer or art director, overseeing other designers and client campaigns. Some become creative directors or run their own studios.

  • ✓University degree in visual arts with specialization in graphic design (or commercial art / graphic communications) OR completion of a college diploma program in graphic arts, required (Job Bank / NOC 52120)

  • ○Proficiency with Adobe Creative Suite (Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign)

Education time

2–4 years
  • •Postsecondary: 4-year university degree in visual arts/graphic design OR 2–3 year college diploma in graphic arts
  • •Portfolio: built throughout school via class projects, internships, and freelance work

Mandatory: A degree or college diploma plus a portfolio is required (per NOC). No general government licence is needed; only the 'Registered Graphic Designer' title is protected in Ontario.

Optional: The RGD designation is optional and mainly relevant for designers who want the protected title.

Job demand

Very limited

Job Bank reports a labour surplus (more workers than openings); employment decline is expected and few positions will come from retirements.

Stress level

Moderate

Client deadlines and revision cycles are the main pressures, designs are subjective, so multiple rounds of changes under tight timelines are normal. Freelancers add income uncertainty.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Agency work can mean overtime before big launches; freelancers control their schedules but income can be irregular.

Flexibility

  • Remote workCommon, design work is easily done remote or hybrid.
  • SchedulingGenerally flexible, though client deadlines set the pace.
  • FreelanceVery common, a large share of graphic designers freelance or take contract work.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Create layouts for print and digital media
  • Design logos, branding, and marketing materials
  • Prepare files for print or web production
  • Meet with clients to understand design briefs
  • Revise designs based on client feedback
  • Keep designs consistent with brand guidelines
  • Juggle multiple projects and deadlines
