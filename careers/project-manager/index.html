Project Manager

A project manager plans, organizes, and leads a project from start to finish, making sure it is completed on time, on budget, and to the required quality. They coordinate the team, manage risks and problems, and keep everyone, from workers to executives, informed. Project managers work in almost every industry, including tech, construction, healthcare, finance, and government.

What does a Project Manager do?

Most project managers work in offices or remotely, but the setting depends heavily on the industry: construction and engineering PMs regularly visit job sites, while IT, finance, and marketing PMs work mostly at desks. Travel to client sites and stakeholder meetings is common.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school

Focus on English, math (functions, data management), business studies, and computer science. Build leadership experience through clubs, teams, student council, or volunteering to organize events, coordinating people and deadlines is the core of the job.

Bachelor's degree (4 years)

Most employers expect a bachelor's degree, usually in a field related to the industry you want to manage projects in, e.g., business, engineering, computer science, or construction management.

Enter via industry experience (1–3 years)

Most project managers do not start as project managers. Common entry points are project coordinator or project assistant roles, or hands-on work in the industry (e.g., software development, construction, marketing) plus co-op or internship experience.

Build project leadership experience (2–4 years)

Take on progressively larger responsibility: running small projects, leading workstreams, managing budgets and schedules, and working with clients and vendors.

Optional certification

The CAPM (Certified Associate in Project Management) suits early-career candidates. The PMP (Project Management Professional) from PMI is the widely recognized credential for experienced managers and requires 36 months of project leadership experience with a bachelor's degree (or 60 months without one), plus 35 hours of project management education.

Advancement

Progress to senior project manager, then program manager (overseeing multiple related projects), PMO lead, or director-level roles, or work independently as a contract consultant.

Common qualifications
  • ○Bachelor's degree in business, engineering, computer science, construction management, or another field related to the industry
  • ○Several years of relevant industry experience, often starting in project coordinator or project assistant roles
  • ○Demonstrated experience leading project work, managing budgets and schedules, and coordinating teams or vendors
Optional qualifications
  • +PMP (Project Management Professional) certification from PMI, widely recognized, often preferred by employers
  • +CAPM (Certified Associate in Project Management), entry-level PMI credential
  • +Agile/Scrum certifications (e.g., Certified ScrumMaster) for tech and software project roles
  • +Industry-specific credentials (e.g., Gold Seal Certification for construction in Canada)
Licensing: No government licence is legally required to work as a project manager in Ontario. The PMP certification from the Project Management Institute is the most widely recognized professional credential but remains optional; many employers list it as preferred rather than mandatory.

Stress level

High

Project managers carry direct accountability for delivering on scope, schedule, and budget under fixed deadlines, often while juggling multiple concurrent projects and managing competing stakeholder demands. Crunch periods around milestones and go-live dates commonly bring long hours and high pressure.

Work-life balance

Challenging

Balance varies a lot by industry and employer: periods of intense work around deadlines, launches, or site milestones alternate with steadier stretches. Remote and contract roles can offer more control over hours, but overall the deadline-driven accountability makes balance harder than in typical 9-to-5 roles.

Flexibility

  • Remote workRemote and hybrid project management roles are common, especially in tech and professional services; many postings are fully remote with occasional travel to client sites.
  • SchedulingFlexible scheduling is frequently offered, particularly in remote and contract roles, though core hours for team coordination are usually expected.
  • FreelanceContract and freelance project management work is common, employers regularly hire PMs on 6- to 12-month contracts, and experienced PMs work as independent consultants.

Recommended preparation (suggestions, not requirements)

High-school courses

  • English
  • Mathematics (Functions, Data Management)
  • Business Studies (entrepreneurship, accounting, or marketing)
  • Computer Science
  • Technological Design or Communications Technology

Experiences & activities

  • Take leadership roles in clubs, teams, or student council to practice coordinating people and deadlines
  • Volunteer to organize events or fundraisers, small-scale project experience
  • Learn a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana, or MS Project) through free tutorials
  • Pursue co-op placements or internships in an industry of interest
  • Read an introductory project management book or take a free online PM fundamentals course
  • Build teamwork and public-speaking skills through debate, drama, or team sports

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Define project scope, goals, deliverables, and success criteria with stakeholders
  • Develop detailed project schedules, budgets, and resource plans
  • Assign tasks, lead project teams, and run status meetings
  • Monitor progress against the plan and take corrective action when work slips
  • Identify, assess, and manage project risks, issues, and changes in scope
  • Communicate status, risks, and decisions to clients, executives, and team members
  • Manage contracts, vendors, and procurement
  • Close out projects, document lessons learned, and hand over deliverables

Where it can lead

  • Project coordinator / project assistant (entry point)
  • Project manager
  • Senior project manager
  • Program manager or PMO (Project Management Office) lead
  • Director of project management / VP operations, or independent contract consultant
