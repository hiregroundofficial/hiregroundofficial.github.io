Cybersecurity Analyst

Cybersecurity analysts protect computer systems and data from hackers and attacks. They monitor networks for suspicious activity, respond to security incidents, and set up defenses to keep organizations' information safe.

What does a Cybersecurity Analyst do?

Analysts work in offices or security operations centres at banks, government departments, and tech firms, or remotely. The leading employers are computer systems design services, finance/insurance, and public administration.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Take computer science, math, and English. Try cybersecurity clubs, capture-the-flag competitions, or a home lab to learn how networks and security work.

Postsecondary Education

Employers usually require a bachelor's degree in computer science, computer security, computer systems engineering, or information systems, or completion of a college program in information technology, network administration, or a related field.

Gain Experience and Certifications

Many analysts start in IT support or network roles first. Certifications or training from software vendors may be required by some employers; professional certifications such as CISSP, CISA, and CISM are common in job postings (optional, not legally required).

Entry-Level Position

Start as a security analyst or SOC (security operations centre) analyst monitoring alerts and responding to incidents.

Career Progression

Advance to senior analyst, security engineer, or security consultant. With deep experience, move into leadership roles such as security manager or CISO (chief information security officer).

Mandatory: A degree or college program is typically required by employers. No government licence is needed, this occupation is not regulated in Canada.

Optional: Security certifications (e.g. CISSP, CISA, CISM) are optional but frequently requested by employers and grow in importance with seniority.

Job demand

Limited

Stress level

High

Analysts respond to live attacks and incidents, often on call outside normal hours. Mistakes can mean data breaches costing organizations millions, and attackers constantly change tactics, so the pressure to stay sharp is high.

Work-life balance

Challenging

On-call rotations and urgent incident response can interrupt evenings and weekends. Many analysts find the work meaningful, but boundaries can be hard to protect.

Flexibility

  • Remote workAvailable in some roles (hybrid/remote monitoring), but on-call availability is usually required.
  • SchedulingShift or on-call rotations are common, including nights and weekends for some teams.
  • FreelancePossible, security consulting and contract work exist, especially with experience.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Monitor networks for suspicious activity
  • Investigate and respond to security incidents
  • Run vulnerability scans and assess risks
  • Configure firewalls and security tools
  • Write incident reports
  • Keep up with new threats and attack methods
  • Educate staff on security best practices
