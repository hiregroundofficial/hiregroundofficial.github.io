Physiotherapist

Physiotherapists help people recover from injuries, manage chronic pain, and improve movement through exercise programs, manual therapy, and rehabilitation techniques. About 14,850 work in Ontario, mostly in private clinics (62%) and hospitals (32%). About 40% are self-employed. You do not need a doctor's referral to see one in Ontario.

What does a Physiotherapist do?

Mostly private physiotherapy clinics (62%) and hospitals (32%). Work is physically active and hands-on, with one-on-one sessions and small teams in clinical settings.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Senior biology, physics, chemistry, and math. Volunteer or work in a physio clinic, sports team, or rehabilitation setting.

Undergraduate Degree

Complete a 4-year bachelor's degree, usually in kinesiology, health sciences, or life sciences.

Master's Degree

Complete a Master of Science in Physical Therapy (MScPT/MPT, ~2 years) from an accredited Canadian university, including clinical placements in hospitals.

Entry-to-Practice Exams

Pass the Physiotherapy Competency Examination (PCE) and the Ontario Clinical Exam (OCE) through the Canadian Alliance of Physiotherapy Regulators.

Registration

Register with the College of Physiotherapists of Ontario (CPO), obtain liability insurance, and begin practising, often with a supervised residency period.

Career Progression

Specialize (sports, orthopedics, pediatrics, neurology), open your own clinic, teach, or pursue research.

Licensing: A certificate of registration from the College of Physiotherapists of Ontario is required to practise and use the title physiotherapist. Requirements include an accredited physiotherapy degree and successful completion of the entry-to-practice examinations.

Job demand

Moderate

Employment growth will create a moderate number of new positions; not many openings from retirements. A growing and aging population generates demand for rehabilitation services.

Stress level

Moderate

Moderate stress from physically demanding work (lifting and supporting patients), emotional load of treating patients in pain, and caseload pressure, balanced by regular daytime schedules and one-on-one patient time.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Most clinic work follows regular daytime hours, and 39% are self-employed with control over their schedules, but many patients need evening or weekend appointments, and part-year work is common.

Flexibility

  • Remote workVery limited, treatment is hands-on and in-person. Virtual consultations exist but are a niche.
  • SchedulingGood flexibility, especially for the self-employed; part-time work is common (22% part-time).
  • FreelanceVery common, about 40% of Ontario physiotherapists are self-employed, including clinic owners and contractors.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Assess patients' movement, strength, and pain
  • Diagnose musculoskeletal and mobility problems
  • Design personalized rehabilitation and exercise programs
  • Deliver hands-on manual therapy treatments
  • Teach patients exercises for home practice
  • Use equipment such as ultrasound and exercise machines
  • Track patient progress and adjust treatment plans
  • Educate patients on injury prevention

Where it can lead

  • Physiotherapist (resident/provisional, then independent)
  • Senior physiotherapist
  • Specialized physiotherapist (sports, neuro, pediatrics)
  • Clinic owner, manager, or director
  • Researcher, educator, or consultant
