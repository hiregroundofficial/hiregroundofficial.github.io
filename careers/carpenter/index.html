Carpenter

Carpenters build and repair the wooden (and sometimes steel) structures of buildings, framing walls and roofs, installing doors, windows, floors, and cabinets. They measure, cut, and assemble materials on construction sites and renovation projects.

What does a Carpenter do?

Residential and commercial construction sites, renovation projects, and workshops. Mostly outdoors and exposed to weather, with work at heights on framing, roofing, and scaffolding.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma with math (geometry is especially useful), English, and construction technology courses. OYAP or co-op placements count toward apprenticeship hours.

Entry Route

Most carpenters start as construction labourers or helpers and learn on the job; others take a college carpentry or renovation techniques program first.

Apprenticeship (Optional but Valuable)

The General Carpenter (403A) apprenticeship is about 7,200 hours, roughly 6,480 on the job and 720 in school over three levels, typically about 4 years.

Certification

Carpentry is a non-compulsory trade: no licence is legally required to work. Passing the Certificate of Qualification exam earns a Red Seal-endorsed credential that improves pay and mobility across provinces.

Career Progression

Carpenters can specialize (framing, finishing, cabinetmaking, formwork), become lead hands or forepersons, start their own renovation or contracting businesses, a very common path, or become construction estimators and site supervisors.

Required qualifications
  • ✓No licence is legally required to work as a carpenter in Ontario, General Carpenter (403A) is a non-compulsory trade (Skilled Trades Ontario). Employers set their own requirements.
Common qualifications
  • ○Ontario Secondary School Diploma (Grade 10 is the minimum apprenticeship entry; most employers prefer Grade 12)
  • ○Completion of the 403A apprenticeship or a college carpentry program
  • ○Fall protection and other construction safety training (e.g. Working at Heights)
Optional qualifications
  • +Certificate of Qualification with Red Seal endorsement, optional, improves pay and interprovincial mobility
  • +Specialized tickets (scaffolding, forklift, first aid)

Stress level

Moderate

The work is physically demanding, heavy lifting, climbing, repetitive motion, and mostly outdoors in all weather. Construction deadlines create time pressure, and mistakes in measurements or structural work are costly to fix.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Hours follow construction schedules and are usually daytime, but overtime is common near deadlines and some residential work is seasonal, with winter slowdowns in some regions.

Flexibility

  • Remote workNot possible, the work is hands-on at physical sites.
  • SchedulingDaytime construction hours; some seasonal variation.
  • FreelanceCommon, self-employment as a renovation or framing contractor is a well-established path.

Experiences & activities

  • Take high school construction and woodworking courses
  • Join OYAP or a co-op placement in carpentry
  • Build personal projects (furniture, sheds) to develop skills
  • Apply for labourer or helper jobs with framing crews or renovation companies

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Read blueprints and plan the day's construction tasks
  • Measure, mark, and cut lumber and sheet materials
  • Frame walls, floors, and roof structures
  • Install windows, doors, stairs, and trim
  • Build and strip concrete formwork on larger projects
  • Repair or replace damaged structural elements in renovations
  • Keep the site safe, tidy, and on schedule

Where it can lead

  • Construction Labourer / Carpenter's Helper
  • Carpenter (403A)
  • Lead Hand / Senior Carpenter
  • Foreperson or Site Supervisor
  • Renovation Contractor or Business Owner
