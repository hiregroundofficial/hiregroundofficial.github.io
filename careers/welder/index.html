Welder

Welders join metal parts together using intense heat from torches, arcs, or lasers. They work from blueprints to cut, shape, and fuse metal for everything from car frames and bridges to custom railings and industrial machinery.

What does a Welder do?

Fabrication shops, manufacturing plants (auto parts, machinery, metal products), construction sites, and repair shops. Hot, noisy, and dirty conditions with mandatory protective equipment, helmet, gloves, respirator, and fire-resistant clothing.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma with math, science, and English. Manufacturing or welding shop courses and a co-op placement build useful hands-on skills.

Entry Route

Many welders start as shop helpers or take a college welding techniques program, then get hired by a fabrication shop or manufacturer.

Apprenticeship (Optional but Valuable)

The Welder (456A) apprenticeship is about 6,000 hours, roughly 5,280 on the job and 720 in school over three levels, typically about 3 years.

Certification

Welding is a non-compulsory trade: no licence is legally required to work. Passing the Certificate of Qualification exam earns a Red Seal-endorsed credential that improves pay and mobility. Many employers also ask for Canadian Welding Bureau (CWB) tickets for specific processes and positions.

Career Progression

Welders can specialize (pressure systems, underwater, robotic welding), become welding inspectors or supervisors, or move into fabrication shop management.

Required qualifications
  • ✓No licence is legally required to work as a welder in Ontario, Welder (456A) is a non-compulsory trade (Skilled Trades Ontario). Employers set their own requirements.
Common qualifications
  • ○Ontario Secondary School Diploma or equivalent (typical employer and apprenticeship expectation)
  • ○Completion of the 456A apprenticeship or a college welding program
  • ○CWB (Canadian Welding Bureau) certifications for specific welding processes and positions, frequently requested by employers
Optional qualifications
  • +Certificate of Qualification with Red Seal endorsement, optional, improves pay and interprovincial mobility
  • +Pressure Systems Welder (456P) certification, a separate voluntary trade in Ontario
  • +Underwater or robotic welding specializations

Stress level

Moderate

Work involves heat, sparks, noise, vibration, and welding fumes, plus awkward or uncomfortable body positions for long stretches, protective gear and strict safety procedures are constant requirements. Precision matters because weak welds can fail with serious consequences.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Manufacturing welders often work shifts, including evenings or weekends; construction welding follows project schedules. Overtime is common during busy periods.

Flexibility

  • Remote workNot possible, the work is hands-on with physical equipment.
  • SchedulingShift work in manufacturing; project-based hours in construction.
  • FreelancePossible, contract and mobile welding work exists, though most welders are employees.

  • Take high school welding or manufacturing shop courses
  • Complete a college welding techniques program
  • Practice striking and running clean beads on scrap metal
  • Apply for helper or labourer jobs at fabrication shops to get a foot in the door

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Read blueprints and welding symbols to plan the work
  • Cut, grind, and fit metal parts before welding
  • Weld joints using MIG, TIG, or stick processes
  • Inspect finished welds for cracks, porosity, and proper penetration
  • Maintain welding equipment and replace consumables
  • Follow safety procedures for fumes, fire, and eye protection
  • Meet production or project deadlines without sacrificing weld quality

Where it can lead

  • Welding Helper / Shop Labourer
  • Welder (456A)
  • Certified / Specialized Welder (CWB tickets, pressure systems)
  • Welding Inspector or Supervisor
  • Fabrication Shop Lead or Owner
