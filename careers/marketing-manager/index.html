Marketing Manager

Marketing managers lead the team that decides how a company presents its products or services to the public. They plan advertising and digital campaigns, set budgets, track results, and manage a team of marketing staff. They work for companies, ad agencies, government departments, and consulting firms, and most reach this role after a few years of hands-on marketing experience.

What does a Marketing Manager do?

Marketing managers mostly work in offices at companies, advertising/PR agencies, or government departments, often coordinating with clients, vendors, and creative teams. About 94% work full-time, and the role blends desk work (planning, analytics, budget reviews) with meetings and occasional event or client-site work.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High school (grades 9–12)

Take English, math, business/entrepreneurship, and computer studies. Join student council, run a club social media page, or organize a school fundraiser to build real marketing experience early.

Bachelor's degree (about 4 years)

Complete a university degree in business administration or commerce with a marketing specialization, or in communications, public relations, or journalism. Co-op or internships during the degree are the fastest way to land entry-level marketing jobs.

Entry-level marketing roles (2–4 years)

Work as a marketing coordinator, marketing specialist, social media coordinator, or sales/marketing representative. Learn to run campaigns, manage budgets, and measure results, this experience is what employers require before promotion to management.

Promotion to marketing manager

After several years of experience in marketing, advertising, public relations, or a related occupation, move into a manager role: leading a team, owning a budget, and being accountable for campaign results.

Ongoing professional development

Keep skills current in digital marketing, analytics, and AI-assisted tools. Optional certifications (Google Ads, HubSpot, Chartered Marketer) and later a senior manager or director role can follow with further experience.

Required qualifications
  • ✓A university degree or college diploma in communications, public relations, marketing, journalism, or a related field, plus several years of experience in an advertising, public relations, or communications officer position or related occupation
  • ✓Or: a university degree or college diploma in business administration (or related field) with a specialization in sales or marketing, plus several years of experience as a sales, marketing, or public relations representative or related occupation
Common qualifications
  • ○Google Analytics / Google Ads certifications
  • ○HubSpot or Salesforce (CRM) training and experience
  • ○A portfolio showing campaigns you planned and measurable results
  • ○Strong skills in marketing analytics and budget management
Optional qualifications
  • +Master of Business Administration (MBA) with a marketing concentration
  • +Chartered Marketer (CM) professional designation
  • +Project Management Professional (PMP) certification
Licensing: No licence is required to work as a marketing manager in Ontario. Job Bank lists only education and experience requirements for this occupation, with no mandatory licence or professional registration.

Stress level

Moderate

Marketing managers carry responsibility for budgets, campaign performance targets, and launch deadlines, and they are answerable when results fall short. Stress spikes around major launches, end-of-quarter reporting, and tight campaign timelines, but the pace between campaigns is generally manageable.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Most marketing managers work standard weekday hours, but campaign launches, events, and tight deadlines periodically require evenings or weekends. The balance is better at established companies with set schedules and harder at agencies juggling many clients at once.

Flexibility

  • Remote workRemote and hybrid work are common, especially in agencies, tech, media, and professional-services roles; on-site presence is more often required for brand retail or event-heavy positions.
  • SchedulingGenerally standard weekday hours, with occasional extended hours around campaign launches, product launches, and events.
  • FreelanceFreelance and contract work exist (consulting, fractional CMO roles), but most positions are permanent full-time employment, 94% of Ontario workers in this occupation are full-time.

Recommended preparation (suggestions, not requirements)

High-school courses

  • English (writing and communication)
  • Mathematics (data handling and analytics)
  • Business Studies / Entrepreneurship
  • Computer Studies / Digital Media
  • Visual Arts or Design
  • French (useful for roles with national employers)

Experiences & activities

  • Run a school club's or team's social media account to practice real campaign planning
  • Complete a free Google Digital Marketing or Google Analytics certificate
  • Do a co-op or internship in a marketing or communications role during university
  • Build a portfolio documenting any campaigns or projects you contributed to
  • Follow marketing industry news and case studies to learn how campaigns succeed or fail
  • Develop public-speaking and presentation skills through clubs or part-time work

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Plan and launch advertising, digital, and social media campaigns
  • Set and manage the marketing budget, tracking spending and return on investment
  • Supervise and mentor marketing coordinators, specialists, and creative staff
  • Analyze campaign performance data and prepare reports for senior management
  • Oversee brand consistency across ads, websites, and public communications
  • Work with agencies, designers, and media vendors on campaign production
  • Research customer needs and competitor activity to guide marketing strategy
  • Coordinate product launches, promotions, and public events

Where it can lead

  • Marketing Coordinator / Marketing Specialist (entry level)
  • Senior Marketing Specialist or Team Lead (2–4 years of experience)
  • Marketing Manager (current career; owns budget and team)
  • Senior Marketing Manager or Marketing Director (manages multiple teams or regions)
  • Vice-President of Marketing / Chief Marketing Officer (executive leadership)
