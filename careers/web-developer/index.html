Web Developer

Web developers build and maintain websites, everything from online stores to news sites. They write the code that makes pages look right and work properly, connecting what users see with the data and services behind the scenes.

What does a Web Developer do?

Developers work at computers in offices at agencies, tech firms, and retailers, or from home. About half work in computer systems design and related services; others work for government or banks. Freelancers work wherever they choose.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Take computer science, math, and English. Start building simple websites with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, free tutorials make this one of the easiest careers to try early.

Postsecondary Education

Employers usually require a bachelor's degree (often in computer science, web development, or software engineering) or completion of a college program in computer science or a related field. Some web developers are self-taught with a strong portfolio, but formal education is the common route.

Gain Experience

Build a portfolio of real websites. Experience as a computer programmer is usually required, so internships, freelance gigs, or co-op terms help a lot.

Entry-Level Position

Start as a junior or front-end developer building pages and fixing bugs, often working in Agile teams.

Career Progression

Grow into full-stack developer (front-end and back-end), senior developer, or lead roles. Some move into UX, product management, or start their own freelance practice.

  • •Portfolio and experience: usually built during school, co-op, or freelance work

Mandatory: A degree or college program completion plus programming experience is typically required by employers. No government licence is needed, this occupation is not regulated in Canada.

Optional: Full-stack skills and platform certifications are optional but improve employability.

Job demand

Very limited

Employment decline is expected as advanced software simplifies web development tasks; several unemployed workers with recent experience are competing for openings.

Stress level

Moderate

Client and launch deadlines create busy periods, and broken websites need fast fixes. Freelancers add the pressure of finding clients and managing their own schedules.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Standard hours are common for employees; crunch happens around launches. Freelancers set their own hours but can struggle to switch off.

Flexibility

  • Remote workVery common, web development is one of the most remote-friendly careers.
  • SchedulingOften flexible, especially in freelance or hybrid roles.
  • FreelanceVery common, many web developers freelance or take contract projects.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Build responsive web pages from designs
  • Write and test front-end code (HTML, CSS, JavaScript)
  • Connect websites to back-end services and APIs
  • Test sites across browsers and devices
  • Fix bugs and performance issues
  • Update website content and features
  • Work with designers and clients on requirements
