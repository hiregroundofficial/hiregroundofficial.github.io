Elementary School Teacher

Elementary school teachers educate children from kindergarten to Grade 8. They plan lessons, teach core subjects like reading, writing, and math, manage classrooms, assess student progress, and work with parents to support each child's learning.

What does an Elementary School Teacher do?

Classrooms in elementary schools, plus schoolyards, gyms, libraries, and field-trip settings. Highly people-facing work with children roughly ages 4–12, alongside other teachers and support staff.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma. Volunteer with children (camps, tutoring, coaching) and keep your grades strong for university admission.

University Degree

Complete at least a 3-year postsecondary degree, most candidates complete a 4-year honours degree, often with teachable subjects like English, math, or science.

Teacher Education Program

Complete a 4-semester teacher education program (e.g. a Bachelor of Education), available as a consecutive program after your degree or concurrently with it.

OCT Certification

Apply to the Ontario College of Teachers for certification and maintain annual membership in good standing. This is legally required to teach in publicly funded schools.

Career Progression

Most new teachers start on occasional (supply) teacher lists, then move into long-term occasional and permanent contract positions. Additional Qualifications open doors to leadership roles like vice-principal and principal.

Required qualifications
  • ✓Ontario College of Teachers (OCT) certification, legally required to teach in publicly funded schools
  • ✓Minimum 3-year postsecondary degree
  • ✓Completion of a 4-semester teacher education program (e.g. Bachelor of Education)
Common qualifications
  • ○Bachelor of Education (B.Ed.) degree, the usual way to complete the teacher education program
  • ○French-language proficiency, significantly improves hiring prospects in Ontario (Job Bank)
  • ○Special education or ESL qualifications, in demand in many boards
Optional qualifications
  • +Additional Qualifications (AQ) courses, required for leadership roles, useful for specialization
  • +Master's degree in education, for leadership or specialist roles
Licensing: Certification from the Ontario College of Teachers (OCT) is legally required to teach in Ontario's publicly funded schools. Teachers in private schools are not legally required to be certified, though many employers prefer it.

Stress level

Moderate

Managing a classroom of 20+ children all day is emotionally and physically demanding, with responsibility for their safety and learning. Lesson planning, marking, report cards, and parent communication extend the workday well beyond school hours.

Work-life balance

Moderate

The school-day schedule and summer break are major perks, but evenings and weekends are routinely spent on lesson planning, marking, and report cards, especially early in a career.

Flexibility

  • Remote workRare, some online teaching and tutoring roles exist, but the job is classroom-based.
  • SchedulingFixed to the school calendar; summers typically off, but evenings and weekends are spent planning and marking.
  • FreelancePossible, private tutoring is a common side income.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Plan daily lessons aligned with the Ontario curriculum
  • Teach core subjects: reading, writing, math, science, and social studies
  • Manage classroom behaviour and create a positive learning environment
  • Assess student work and write report cards
  • Communicate with parents about student progress
  • Supervise students at recess, lunch, and on field trips
  • Differentiate instruction for students with different needs and abilities

Where it can lead

  • Teacher Candidate (B.Ed. student)
  • Occasional / Supply Teacher
  • Permanent Elementary Teacher
  • Department Head or Lead Teacher
  • Vice-Principal / Principal (requires additional qualifications)
