IT Support Specialist

IT support specialists are the tech problem-solvers people call when computers, software, or networks stop working. They diagnose issues, walk users through fixes, and keep workplaces running smoothly, often as the first line of technical help.

What does an IT Support Specialist do?

Support specialists work in offices, IT departments, call centres, or on-site at client workplaces across every industry, from hospitals to banks to schools. The work mixes desk-based troubleshooting with in-person help.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Take computer science and math. Practice by fixing family and friends' computer problems, hands-on troubleshooting is exactly what this job is.

Postsecondary Education

This occupation usually requires a college diploma in computer science or a related field (or an apprenticeship of 2+ years). This is one of the fastest routes into tech.

Certifications (Optional)

Entry-level vendor certifications such as CompTIA A+ can help. In Ontario, two voluntary skilled trades are associated with this occupation: IT Contact Centre Technical Support Agent and Hardware Technician.

Entry-Level Position

Start on a help desk (Tier 1), answering support requests by phone, chat, or in person and solving common hardware/software problems.

Career Progression

Move up to senior support or Tier 3 specialist roles, then into system administration, network administration, or IT management. Many IT professionals start their careers in support.

Job demand

Limited

Stress level

High

Help-desk roles involve constant interruptions, queues of waiting users, and time pressure to resolve tickets quickly. Dealing with frustrated people all day is emotionally demanding, and some roles include shift work in call centres.

Work-life balance

Moderate

Many roles keep standard hours, but call-centre and shift schedules (evenings/weekends) are common, which can disrupt routines.

Flexibility

  • Remote workAvailable in some roles, remote help-desk and chat-support positions exist.
  • SchedulingMay include shifts, evenings, or weekend coverage, especially in call centres.
  • FreelanceRare, most support roles are full-time employee positions.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Respond to user support requests by phone, chat, or in person
  • Diagnose hardware and software problems
  • Walk users through step-by-step fixes
  • Install and configure software and devices
  • Document solutions in a ticketing system
  • Escalate complex issues to senior technicians
  • Set up accounts and access for new employees

Where it can lead

  • Help Desk Technician (Tier 1)
  • IT Support Specialist (Tier 2/3)
  • System Administrator or Network Administrator
  • IT Manager
