Lawyer

Lawyers advise clients on their legal rights and represent them in negotiations, transactions, and court. They research the law, draft contracts and legal documents, argue cases before judges, and help people and businesses solve disputes, from real estate deals to criminal defence.

What does a Lawyer do?

Law firm offices, corporate legal departments, government offices, legal aid clinics, and courtrooms. A mix of desk-based research and writing, client meetings, negotiations, and court appearances.

How to get there, step by step

The typical route into this career in Ontario. Required steps are marked, everything else is common or optional.

High School

Earn your Ontario Secondary School Diploma with strong English, history, and social science courses. Debate club and mock trial build useful skills.

Undergraduate Degree

Complete a bachelor's degree (usually 4 years) in any field, strong grades and a competitive LSAT score are needed for law school admission.

Law School

Earn a 3-year Juris Doctor (J.D.) from an accredited Canadian law school (or equivalent legal education).

Licensing

Complete the Law Society of Ontario's Lawyer Licensing Process: the barrister and solicitor licensing examinations plus experiential training, typically about 10 months of articling or the Law Practice Program.

Career Progression

Get called to the bar and begin practising as an associate. Career paths lead to partnership, in-house counsel roles, government legal work, or the judiciary.

Stress level

High

Lawyers handle high-stakes problems, people's liberty, money, families, and businesses, with hard court and filing deadlines. Billable-hour targets, adversarial proceedings, and the expectation of near-perfect work create sustained pressure.

Work-life balance

Challenging

Long hours are the norm in private practice, with billable-hour targets and unpredictable client emergencies and court deadlines. Government and in-house roles tend to be more predictable.

Flexibility

  • Remote workIncreasingly common, many firms offer hybrid work, though court appearances and client meetings are in person.
  • SchedulingDemanding, long hours and unpredictable client or court deadlines are normal, especially in private practice.
  • FreelanceCommon, about 40% of lawyers are self-employed (Job Bank), often as sole practitioners or in small firms.

What a typical day actually looks like

Concrete responsibilities, not one person's story, but the common core of the job.

  • Meet with clients to understand their legal problems
  • Research laws, regulations, and past court decisions
  • Draft contracts, wills, pleadings, and legal opinions
  • Negotiate settlements on behalf of clients
  • Represent clients in court, tribunals, or mediations
  • Keep up with changes in the law through continuing education
  • Manage case files and meet court and filing deadlines

Where it can lead

  • Associate Lawyer
  • Senior Associate / Partner
  • In-House Counsel, Judge, or Firm Founder
