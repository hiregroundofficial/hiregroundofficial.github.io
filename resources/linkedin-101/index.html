LinkedIn 101

Building Your Professional Brand Online

Your network is a garden: plant early, water often.
Essential skill10 min read

Why Is LinkedIn Important?

LinkedIn is the world's largest professional networking platform, like a mix between a living resume, a portfolio, and a social network for your career. Employers and recruiters use it to search for candidates, students use it to find mentors and internships, and professionals use it to share ideas and stay in touch.

LinkedIn is important because it makes you visible. Recruiters regularly search the platform using keywords, so a complete profile can put you in front of employers you would never have reached otherwise. Many opportunities are found through people you know, or people who know them, rather than through job postings. A strong LinkedIn presence lets opportunities find you, even while you are busy with school or work.

Key Terms

Profile
Your public professional page on LinkedIn, which showcases your education, skills, and experience.
Headline
The short line under your name that describes who you are and what you do.
Connection
Someone you are linked to on LinkedIn. 1st-degree connections are people you are directly connected with.
Network
The collection of people you know, plus the people they know.
Endorsement
A one-click confirmation from a connection that you have a particular skill.
Recommendation
A short written testimonial from a teacher, manager, or colleague about your work.
Feed
The stream of posts, articles, and updates from your connections and the people you follow.
Custom URL
A personalized web address for your profile (e.g., linkedin.com/in/yourname).
Informational Interview
A casual conversation with a professional to learn about their career path, not to ask for a job.
Personal Brand
The impression people have of your skills, values, and personality based on what they see of you.

LinkedIn vs. Resume

FeatureResumeLinkedIn Profile
Length1–2 pages, tightly editedNo strict limit; more detail allowed
TailoringCustomized for each jobGeneral, for a wide audience
ToneFormal and conciseProfessional but more personal (first person is fine)
ExtrasNot usually includedRecommendations, projects, links, posts, and a photo
NetworkingNoneConnect, message, and follow people and companies
Tip: Your resume and LinkedIn should match on facts such as dates, job titles, and schools. Inconsistencies can look careless.

Building Your Profile

Profile photo and banner: Choose a clear, recent photo where your face is easy to see. It does not need to be taken by a professional: a friendly smile, good lighting, and a plain background are enough. Dress as you would for an interview. Avoid group photos, filters, sunglasses, or cropped-out party pictures. A simple banner image related to your field adds polish.

Headline: Your headline appears next to your name everywhere on LinkedIn, so it matters. Instead of leaving it as just “Student,” add keywords and value. A good formula is: Role/Identity | Key Skills or Interests | Goal.

Basic HeadlineStronger Headline
StudentBusiness Student at Example University | Marketing, Social Media & Event Planning | Seeking Summer 2027 Internship
Looking for workCustomer Service Professional | Bilingual (English/French) | Reliable Team Player
Grade 12 StudentHigh School Student | Robotics Club Captain | Passionate About Engineering & Problem-Solving

The About section is your chance to tell your story in your own voice. Aim for 3–4 short paragraphs: (1) who you are and what you are passionate about, (2) your key skills and accomplishments, and (3) what you are looking for or how people can contact you. Write in the first person (“I”), keep paragraphs short, and include keywords from the fields you are interested in.

Experience, education, and skills:

  • Experience: List jobs, volunteer roles, and leadership positions using the same action-verb bullet points from your resume.
  • Education: Include your school, program, and expected graduation date. Add clubs, awards, or relevant courses.
  • Skills: Add 10–15 relevant skills, with the most important ones at the top. Skills help recruiters find you.
  • Projects, certifications, and volunteering: Great for students with limited work experience. Add links or files when possible.
  • Recommendations: Politely ask a teacher, coach, or supervisor to write a short recommendation about you.

Custom URL and settings: Customize your profile URL so it is short and professional (usually your name), and add it to your resume and email signature. Review your privacy settings so you know who can see your profile and activity. Turning on the “Open to Work” feature lets recruiters know you are looking, and you can choose whether everyone or only recruiters can see it.

Growing Your Network

A network is not about collecting hundreds of connections. It is about building genuine relationships. Start with people you already know: classmates, teachers, coworkers, managers, coaches, family friends, and volunteer supervisors. Then expand to alumni, professionals in fields you like, and people you meet at events.

How to Send a Connection Request

Always add a short, personal note instead of using the default message. Here is a template:

“Hi Ms. Patel, I'm a second-year business student at Example University. I really enjoyed your talk on digital marketing at our career fair and would love to stay in touch. Thank you!”

Informational Interviews

Once connected, you can ask professionals for a short 15–20 minute chat about their career. Be polite, specific, and respectful of their time, and never ask for a job directly. Come with questions such as, “What do you like most about your work?” or “What advice would you give someone starting out?” Always send a thank-you message afterward.

Engaging on LinkedIn

You do not have to become an “influencer,” but being active helps people remember you. Some simple ways to engage:

  • Like and thoughtfully comment on posts from people and companies you follow
  • Share updates such as a completed course, a volunteer event, a competition, or a project
  • Follow companies and industry leaders in the fields you are interested in
  • Join groups related to your school, interests, or career goals
Keep it professional: Avoid controversial arguments, complaining about teachers or employers, or anything you would not want a future boss to read.

Using LinkedIn to Find Jobs

  • Use the Jobs tab to search by title and location, and filter for internships, entry-level, part-time, or remote roles.
  • Set Job Alerts so new postings come to you.
  • Look at who works at a company, then reach out to a connection who may be able to share insight.
  • Follow the application instructions carefully. Some postings use “Easy Apply,” while others send you to the company website.

Staying Safe on LinkedIn

  • Never share your Social Insurance Number, banking details, or passwords, even with someone claiming to be a recruiter.
  • Be cautious of job offers that seem too good to be true, ask you to pay a fee, or ask you to move the conversation to an unusual messaging app.
  • Research any company that contacts you and confirm the job is listed on the official company website.
  • Only accept connection requests from people you know or who have a clear professional reason to connect.
  • Report suspicious profiles or messages using LinkedIn's reporting tools.

How to Build Your LinkedIn Profile

  1. Create Your Account

    Sign up with a professional email address and a strong, unique password. Fill in your name, school, and location. Check LinkedIn's current age and account requirements.

  2. Add a Photo and Banner

    Upload a clear, friendly headshot and, if you like, a simple banner image.

  3. Write Your Headline and About Section

    Use keywords, show your value, and write in a natural first-person voice.

  4. Fill In Experience, Education, and Skills

    Add every relevant experience, using strong bullet points. Include volunteering, projects, and awards, and list your top skills.

  5. Build Your Network

    Connect with 20–30 people you already know, then follow companies and professionals in your field. Send personalized requests. This is the most important part. A great profile with no network stays invisible, while an active network keeps your profile in front of the right people.

  6. Review and Maintain

    Proofread everything, customize your URL, and check your privacy settings. Ask a friend or mentor to review your profile. Update it whenever you gain new experience or skills.

And repeat! Spend 10–15 minutes a week on LinkedIn: react to a few posts, reconnect with someone, and update your profile.

Quick Checklist

  • Do I have a clear, professional profile photo?
  • Does my headline include keywords beyond just “Student”?
  • Is my About section written in my own voice and free of errors?
  • Do my dates and titles match my resume?
  • Have I listed at least 10 relevant skills?
  • Have I customized my URL and checked my privacy settings?
  • Am I following companies and connecting with people I know?
